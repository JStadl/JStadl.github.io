My girlfriend and I recently went on an almost 3 month long trip of South East Asia.  We found relatively little useful info for us online, as most advice was geared to either solo backpackers or honeymooners/older adults, and reviews were often obviously paid for or fake so we thought it would be nice to put up our experiences and recommendations as a pair of 22 year olds, travelling in June to September, looking to be frugal but not slumming it.


We budgeted on the order of ~£80 a day for both of us, but this varied throughout the trip. We tried to keep accommodation costs to around £25 per day, which we found really doable. This is the price range where you can get typically a budget hotel room, or a private room in a hostel. We tended to prefer private rooms in hostels, as there are more opportunities to make friends etc. We found that the private rooms at hostels booked out in advance, so we were not very spontaneous with our travels - we tended to have booked our accommodation for the next 2ish weeks on a rolling basis, as we found this caused us the least stress. We used the Wanderlog app to organise beforehand and during and it worked great.

We would overall say we were pretty happy with our itinerary choice. Here are the only weird parts/caveats that others may not want to replicate:

1. The long Da Lat stint - we really liked Da Lat, and we took this because we felt we needed a bit of a break, and it was just so hot in Vietnam and Da Lat is like 10 degrees cooler than the rest of the country. This would only be recommended if you have time and are going at this same hot time of year (July) and are struggling.

2. The long Bali stint was not our original intention, we wanted to take a tour from Bali to go to Mount Bromo and Mount Ijen, and we heard these are good and worth going to, but wildfires prevented us at the time. However, we really liked our time in Bali actually so maybe it is worth considering anyways.

3. The Singapore part was only doable on our budget as we had somewhere to stay, but we did have to orient our time to be there on certain dates. This meant that we cut Thailand somewhat shorter and cut Laos completely, and had quite a long time in Indonesia. We think this was fine overall but note that if you're not tied to dates going through Laos to Vietnam instead of flying might be fun.

4. You may notice that we tend to spend 3+ nights if we can at a place - travelling, especially on a budget, is quite exhausting so for our own pacing we tried to minimize the number of places we went. This worked well for us, by the end we felt good still and didn't have much travel fatigue. But this may just be due to the length of the trip. We heard from other couples that we met that 3 months is the sweet spot, longer than that may get exhausting.

# Where We Stayed and What We Did



For a bit more detail, here's a short review for each accommodation we stayed in, and some highlight activities we enjoyed the most.



## Bangkok (2 nights + sleeper train, 4 nights)



**Accommodation:** 1. Social Pod Khao San private room - it was tied last. Wouldn't recommend. Had ok social but rooms were fairly gross. 2. Luk Hostel: Also quite bad, zero social scene and we had to move rooms because of poor maintenance of the AC units. Wouldn't recommend. Sleeper train to Chiang Mai recommended, but book in advance and must get AC sleeper carriage.



**Activities:** Highly recommend the mango sticky rice at K. Panich. Song Wat was nice to walk around too, braised goose was worth getting but sells out later in the day.



## Chiang Mai (3 nights + 2 nights after Pai)



**Accommodation:** We stayed at the Entaneer Poshtel twice in a private room, and had no complaints. It was nice, atmosphere was slightly down but there weren't many travellers there mainly just digital nomads. Recommended.



**Activities:** We did a cooking class at Zabb E Lee cooking school that was quite fun. We also ate so much khao soi, and pretty much all of it was great, especially at Khao Soi Mae Sai.



## Pai (3 nights)



**Accommodation:** K-Bunk Pai: Liked it here, recommend.



**Activities:** Renting a scooter and going to Pai Canyon/Yun Lai viewpoint.



## Hanoi (4 nights)



**Accommodation:** 1. Hanoi Backpackers Hostel & Rooftop Bar: This place sucked and broke us. The extractor fan fell out of the wall and woke us up during a storm, leaving a gaping hole in the wall. We left early and asked for a refund because it was so terrible. Not recommended. 2. JM Marvel Hotel and Spa: We went here to recuperate, it was out of normal budget so it's kind of cheating but we found it wonderful. Recommended.



**Activities:** Vietnamese Women's Museum was very strong and probably the best one we went to in Vietnam. Food tour with Ella (Ella Hanoi Food Tour) was also great and would recommend.



## Cat Ba (3 nights)



**Accommodation:** We stayed at Eco Stay Cat Ba, which was nice and wholesome. It was far away from everything else and there was nothing to do but it was great to sit by the pool and read books all day. Recommend.



**Activities:** We did nothing here, besides eat at the restaurant and swim. The beef pho and seafood banh mi were the best items.



## Tam Coc/Ninh Binh (3 nights)



**Accommodation:** Tam Coc Hello Homestay. Recommend, was good overall, not a hostel though.



**Activities:** Trang An Grottoes was one of our highlights of the whole trip. Unbelievably beautiful, fun scooter ride to get there. Bai Dinh Pagoda was also great, mainly because it was unbelievably strange. It was like a Buddhist Disneyland. Was very surreal.



## Phong Nha (3 nights)



**Accommodation:** Phong Nha An An Homestay. Really nice, had a family dinner which was great to meet people. Recommend.



**Activities:** Paradise cave and Mooc Spring. We did them one after the other, and was great. The scooter ride to Paradise cave was slightly treacherous but was also beautiful.



## Hue (2 nights)



**Accommodation:** Hue Sweethouse 2 Homestay. Was fine, brand new (stickers still on everything). Fairly standard hotel with limited socialising but would recommend.



**Activities:** Food tour, however we forgot the exact one. Food here is really strange but unique and can be quite good, if you're low on time especially it's great to do a tour.



## Hoi An (5 nights)



**Accommodation:** Cozy Savvy Hoi An. Really great, it was out of budget but we pushed it for our birthdays. Recommend.



**Activities:** Madam Khanh Banh Mi, we had this like 5 times in 3 days. Tailoring at The Vy was also great. We did a coffee making class (at 10C Bà Triệu, Hội An, Đà Nẵng 560000, Vietnam), which we really enjoyed. Worth doing one of these classes but fairly sure they are similar all around the country.



## Da Nang (2 nights + sleeper bus)



**Accommodation:** Rom Casa Da Nang Hostel. It was fine, bang average hostel similar to Europe. Socials were nice. Leaning towards not recommending as it was a bit dirty, and the dorms were not super cheap.



**Activities:** We didn't do much here to be honest. Went out one night but was fairly underwhelming. Ramen at Chitan Ramen was pretty good.



## Da Lat (7 nights)



**Accommodation:** CozyNook Boutique Apartments 3 thang 2 Street. Was great for a relaxing aparthotel. A bit expensive but was a good base. Recommend.



**Activities:** Datanla Coaster was fun. The com tam at Com Tam Co Hai was also wonderful, we went there many times.



## Ho Chi Minh City (2 nights)



**Accommodation:** Mobylette Saigon Hostel. Single beds in dorms were fine/as expected. Bar on roof was really nice. Recommend for what it is.



**Activities:** Pho at Pho Le was great, best we had in Vietnam.



## Phnom Penh (2 nights)



**Accommodation:** Onederz Phnom Penh private room. Good, clean, good price, good location. Recommended.



**Activities:** Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum and Killing Fields with audio guides. Really heavy, but well delivered. Royal palace of Cambodia was also very impressive.



## Siem Reap (5 nights)



**Accommodation:** Onederz Siem Reap private room. Oh it was so great, great social, great food. Recommended.



**Activities:** Angkor, specifically the Ta Prohm ('Tomb Raider') temple. Food at Tevy's place was good too. Artisans Angkor we also recommend.



## Singapore (4 nights)



**Accommodation:** Stayed at friend's house luckily otherwise this definitely wouldn't be in budget.



**Activities:** National Gallery Singapore was well curated, also just going through hawker centers trying anything well rated was so much fun.



## Malacca (2 nights)



**Accommodation:** Yote 28 Hostel. Had double bed dorm rooms which was nice, and cute cats, but room had no window which we realized is a big deal for us. Recommend if you can stand no window.



**Activities:** We found Jonker Street and A Famosa a bit underwhelming but what saved it was Sudu (Bingsu shop) and walking around the canal near it at night. Absolutely amazing.



## Kuala Lumpur (3 nights)



**Accommodation:** Ahyu Hotel. Well priced and clean, but again had no window. Recommend if you can stand no window.



**Activities:** We loved it here, too many to choose. Islamic Arts Museum was great, National Museum was great, right next to the hotel is Mollagaa Restaurant and it was so great we started calling it the curry of life and went there every night.



## Penang (4 nights)



**Accommodation:** Macalister Terraces Hotel. It was nice, but it was slightly pricey and the room still didn't have a window. Honestly hard to recommend for its price point.



**Activities:** Sister Yao's Char Koay Kak was awesome for breakfast, Laksalicious also really good food. Worth trying the durian here too if you can stand it lol.



## Bali (Uluwatu) (8 nights)



**Accommodation:** Karma Backpacker. Was so great with family dinner included every night, super social, great private room, reasonable price. Recommended.



**Activities:** Honestly we were scared thinking Bali was going to be very Instagram/Dubai but we had a great time here. Hanging out at hostel was good, Hatch club was good. Otherwise we did little, reading and swimming at Banana lounge took most of our time.



## Nusa Lembongan (4 nights)



**Accommodation:** Castaway Hostel Nusa Lembongan. Really great, top 3 places we stayed for sure. Great pools, nice private room, still some social.



**Activities:** The snorkeling we booked through the hostel was unreal, absolutely loved it. Watch out that on this island all the food we tried was really salty for some reason.



## Yogyakarta (4 nights)



**Accommodation:** Amaire Jogja. Good, nice included breakfast, nice pool and room. Recommended.



**Activities:** Affandi Museum. Keraton/Royal Palace, especially when the gamelan is playing. Ber.interaksi coffee shop was absolutely outstanding, great coffee, great conversation with the barista, honestly an unexpected highlight of the whole trip.



## Jakarta (3 nights)



**Accommodation:** Ibis Jakarta Raden Saleh. Boring we know but we just wanted something simple before our flight. Was fine. Recommended.



**Activities:** Cathedral and Istiqlal mosque, recommend getting the tourist free tour inside the mosque especially. Anti-recommendation for the National Museum, it was really expensive and terribly put together.

# Tips



**Mixue:** This is a Chinese ice cream chain that has many locations across all the places here described. It is very cheap and good, and we just made a rule that every time we saw one we got an ice cream and it kept us cool and happy.



**Pizza 4Ps:** It may sound very uncultured to go to South East Asia and recommend pizza, but the pizza here is unreal. Slightly more expensive than average in these areas, but still much cheaper than Europe, they seriously have the best service, food, and atmosphere. We went to 3 different locations a total of 5 times, no regrets.



**Dehydrate before long bus rides** - ok not literally but go easy on the water. Assume they will not stop for the bathroom, and if they do it will be after 5+ hours. All the buses we took were fine pretty much, but the bathroom thing is no joke and every time there were foreigners getting caught by this and hating their life.



**Renting scooters:** Honda Air Blade is a standard model that is everywhere and you can't go wrong, for 2 people though a Honda PCX is much more comfortable for you both and has more power that can be necessary.



**Reviews on Hostelworld/Google Maps:** We found there can be quite a lot of review manipulation (people getting free stuff in exchange for good reviews, botting). We found a good counter to this is to sort for reviews lowest to highest, and see if anyone mentions this. If they do, discount the place. But also don't pay attention to all the lowest reviews/sort by default by lowest, because plenty of reviewers are just delusional or stupid and vote low for bad reasons, just use it as a filter that you then keyword scan.
